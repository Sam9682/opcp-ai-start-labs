Guardrails: the Five Layers of Control

Afternoon · Design & Governance ⏱ ~45 min

Guardrails are the controls that bound what an agent may do. The more autonomy you grant (from the morning's spectrum), the stronger the guardrails must be. This lesson presents five complementary layers — think defense in depth, not a single switch.

Learning Objectives

  • Name and explain the five guardrail layers
  • Map each layer to a concrete control on opcp-explorer
  • Combine layers into defense in depth

Defense in Depth

flowchart TD
    L1[1. Permissions] --> L2[2. Operational limits]
    L2 --> L3[3. Human approval]
    L3 --> L4[4. Observability]
    L4 --> L5[5. Kill switch]

Each layer catches what the previous one misses. No single layer is sufficient alone.

Layer 1 — Permissions

What the agent is allowed to touch. Scoped tokens, allow-lists of tools, read-only vs read-write access, environment boundaries (staging vs production). On opcp-explorer: a token scoped to apps:read and apps:write but never billing:*, limited to one project.

Layer 2 — Operational Limits

How much the agent may do. Max loop iterations, retry/error ceilings, token and spend budgets, rate limits, wall-clock timeouts. These are the enforced form of the morning's stopping criteria. On opcp-explorer: "at most 5 deploy attempts, 10 minutes, €X spend."

Layer 3 — Human Approval

Where a person must say yes. A human-in-the-loop checkpoint before high-impact or irreversible actions. On opcp-explorer: the agent may deploy to staging autonomously, but promoting to production requires operator approval.

Layer 4 — Observability

Being able to see what the agent did. Structured execution traces, logs, metrics, and alerts — the trace you annotated this morning. Without observability, the other layers are unverifiable. On opcp-explorer: every tool call and decision is logged with inputs, outputs, and the stopping reason.

Layer 5 — Kill Switch

The ability to stop the agent immediately. A single, reliable control that halts the loop and revokes access, regardless of the agent's state. On opcp-explorer: an operator command that terminates the agent's session and invalidates its token.

The Five Layers at a Glance

LayerQuestion it answersopcp-explorer control
PermissionsWhat may it touch?Scoped token, tool allow-list
Operational limitsHow much may it do?Retry/iteration/spend caps
Human approvalWhat needs a human yes?Approval before production
ObservabilityCan we see what it did?Structured traces and logs
Kill switchCan we stop it now?Session terminate + token revoke
Design tip: set each layer in proportion to autonomy and impact. A low-autonomy, reversible action needs light guardrails; a high-autonomy, irreversible one needs all five.

Key Takeaways

  • Five layers: permissions, operational limits, human approval, observability, kill switch.
  • They work together as defense in depth — no single layer suffices.
  • Scale each layer to the agent's autonomy and the action's impact.
