Human-in-the-Loop Governance and the OVHcloud AI Policy

Afternoon · Design & Governance ⏱ ~30 min

Guardrails are only as good as the governance around them. This lesson covers human-in-the-loop (HITL) as a governance practice and connects it to responsible-AI principles aligned with the OVHcloud AI Policy.

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish human-in-the-loop, on-the-loop, and out-of-the-loop
  • Decide which actions require human review
  • Apply responsible-AI principles aligned with the OVHcloud AI Policy

Three Degrees of Human Oversight

  • Human-in-the-loop — a person approves actions before they execute. Highest control, lowest throughput.
  • Human-on-the-loop — the agent acts autonomously, but a person monitors and can intervene or stop it.
  • Human-out-of-the-loop — no runtime human involvement; control is entirely in the pre-set guardrails.

These map directly onto the autonomy spectrum from the morning: higher autonomy moves you from in-the-loop toward out-of-the-loop, and demands stronger pre-set guardrails to compensate.

Which Actions Need a Human?

Require human review when an action is:

  • Irreversible — deleting data, tearing down infrastructure.
  • High-impact — production changes, spending above a threshold.
  • Low-confidence — the agent is uncertain or the situation is novel.
  • Sensitive — touching personal data, security, or access controls.

Responsible AI and the OVHcloud AI Policy

The OVHcloud AI Policy frames how AI should be built and operated responsibly. The principles below are summarized for this course; always design against your organization's current, canonical policy document.

  • Transparency — it is clear when AI is acting and what it did (ties to observability).
  • Human accountability — a person remains responsible for the agent's actions.
  • Data protection and privacy — respect data residency, minimization, and user rights.
  • Security — least-privilege access and protection against misuse (ties to permissions).
  • Fairness and safety — avoid harmful or discriminatory outcomes.
  • Controllability — a human can always oversee, pause, and stop the system (ties to the kill switch).
Canonical source: this is a teaching summary. Confirm the exact wording and obligations against the official OVHcloud AI Policy published by your organization before relying on it in a design.

From Principles to Controls

Responsible-AI principles are not abstract: each maps to a guardrail layer you can implement. Transparency and accountability map to observability; security and privacy to permissions; controllability to the kill switch and human approval. The afternoon workshops turn these mappings into a concrete design.

Key Takeaways

  • Oversight comes in degrees: in-the-loop, on-the-loop, out-of-the-loop.
  • Require human review for irreversible, high-impact, low-confidence, or sensitive actions.
  • Responsible-AI principles map directly onto the five guardrail layers.
