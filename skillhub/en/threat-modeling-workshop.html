Workshop: Threat Modeling an AI Agent

Afternoon · Workshop ⏱ ~45 min

In this hands-on workshop you threat-model an agent that operates on opcp-explorer and define the guardrails that keep it safe. The output is a completed threat-model deliverable whose mitigations map onto the five guardrail layers.

Learning Objectives

  • Threat-model an opcp-explorer-connected agent
  • Map each mitigation to a guardrail layer
  • Confirm coverage of all five layers via self-assessment

Prerequisites

  • Completed: Guardrails: the Five Layers of Control
  • Completed: Human-in-the-Loop & the OVHcloud AI Policy
  • The trace-reading lab (so you have seen a real failure)

Step 1 — Describe the System

Pick an agent (e.g. a deploy-and-verify agent). Note its goal, its opcp-explorer tools, its autonomy level, and what it can touch. Record this in section 1 of the template.

Step 2 — Find the Trust Boundaries

Where does untrusted input enter? Prompts, tool outputs, repository files, other agents. Each boundary is a place a threat can cross.

Step 3 — Enumerate Threats

Cover at least the three agent-specific threats, grounded in the trace you annotated:

  • Tool misuse — e.g. deploying to production unintentionally.
  • Prompt injection — a repo file telling the agent to exfiltrate a token.
  • Runaway loops — the repeated deploy failures you saw this morning.

Step 4 — Map Mitigations to Guardrail Layers

For each threat, choose a mitigation and assign it to a guardrail layer. Fill the mapping table in the template. Then confirm coverage below.

Deliverable: complete deliverables/threat-model-template.md (or the .fr.md version). Keep the headings; replace the italic prompts.

Self-Assessment: Five-Layer Coverage

Tick a box once your threat model includes at least one mitigation mapped to that guardrail layer. All five are required to complete this workshop.
